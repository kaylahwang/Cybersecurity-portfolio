# Module 2 Glossary - Security Frameworks, Controls, and Risk Management

---

## Asset

**Definition:**  
An item perceived as having value to an organization.

**Examples:**
- Customer data
- Financial information
- Hardware
- Software
- Intellectual property

---

## Attack Surface

**Definition:**  
The total number of potential vulnerabilities or entry points that attackers can exploit.

**Example:**
- Open ports
- Unnecessary services
- Unpatched applications

---

## Attack Vector

**Definition:**  
A pathway or method attackers use to penetrate security defenses.

**Examples:**
- Phishing
- Malware
- Credential theft

---

## Authentication

**Definition:**  
The process of verifying the identity of a person or system.

**Examples:**
- Password
- Multi-factor authentication (MFA)
- Biometrics

---

## Authorization

**Definition:**  
The process of granting access to specific resources after authentication.

Example:

Authentication:
"Who are you?"

Authorization:
"What are you allowed to access?"

---

## Availability

**Definition:**  
The principle that authorized users can access data and systems when needed.

Part of the CIA Triad.

Examples:
- Backup systems
- Disaster recovery
- High availability systems

---

## Biometrics

**Definition:**  
Unique physical characteristics used to verify a person's identity.

Examples:
- Fingerprint
- Facial recognition
- Iris scan

---

## CIA Triad

**Definition:**  
A foundational security model used to establish security policies and systems.

Three principles:

- Confidentiality
- Integrity
- Availability

---

## Confidentiality

**Definition:**  
Ensuring that only authorized users can access specific information.

Security methods:

- Access control
- Authentication
- Least privilege

---

## Cryptography

**Definition:**  
The practice of protecting information by transforming data so unauthorized users cannot read or modify it.

---

## Cyber Threat Framework (CTF)

**Definition:**  
A framework developed by the U.S. government that provides a common language for describing and communicating cyber threat activity.

Purpose:

- Improve threat analysis
- Improve information sharing

---

## Defense in Depth

**Definition:**  
A security strategy that uses multiple layers of security controls.

Example:

Firewall  
+
MFA  
+
Endpoint security  
+
Monitoring

Purpose:

If one control fails, other controls provide protection.

---

## Encryption

**Definition:**  
The process of converting readable data into an encoded format to protect confidentiality and integrity.

Example:

Plaintext → Encrypted data

---

## Framework

**Definition:**  
Guidelines used for building security plans to mitigate risks and threats.

Purpose:

- Provide security direction
- Support compliance
- Manage risk

Examples:

- NIST CSF
- ISO/IEC 27001

---

## Governance

**Definition:**  
The process of establishing cybersecurity strategy, policies, roles, and responsibilities aligned with business goals.

---

## Integrity

**Definition:**  
Ensuring data is correct, authentic, and reliable.

Security methods:

- Cryptography
- Hashing
- Digital signatures

---

## ISO/IEC 27001

**Definition:**  
An internationally recognized information security management framework.

Purpose:

Help organizations manage information security risks.

---

## Least Privilege

**Definition:**  
A security principle where users receive only the minimum access necessary to perform their tasks.

Purpose:

Reduce unauthorized access and limit damage.

---

## Multi-Factor Authentication (MFA)

**Definition:**  
An authentication method requiring two or more verification factors.

Examples:

- Something you know (password)
- Something you have (security token)
- Something you are (biometric)

---

# NIST Cybersecurity Framework (CSF)

**Definition:**  
A voluntary framework consisting of standards, guidelines, and best practices to manage cybersecurity risk.

Six Core Functions:

1. Govern
2. Identify
3. Protect
4. Detect
5. Respond
6. Recover

---

## NIST CSF - Govern

**Definition:**  
A function focused on establishing cybersecurity strategy, policies, roles, and risk management processes.

---

## NIST CSF - Identify

**Definition:**  
A function focused on understanding organizational assets, risks, and policies.

---

## NIST CSF - Protect

**Definition:**  
A function focused on implementing safeguards, policies, procedures, training, and tools.

---

## NIST CSF - Detect

**Definition:**  
A function focused on identifying cybersecurity events and improving monitoring capabilities.

---

## NIST CSF - Respond

**Definition:**  
A function focused on containing, analyzing, and managing security incidents.

---

## NIST CSF - Recover

**Definition:**  
A function focused on restoring affected systems back to normal operation.

---

# OWASP (Open Web Application Security Project)

**Definition:**  
A nonprofit organization focused on improving software security.

Purpose:

- Promote secure development
- Reduce application vulnerabilities

---

## Security Audit

**Definition:**  
A review of an organization's security controls, policies, and procedures against a set of expectations.

Purpose:

- Identify risks
- Evaluate controls
- Improve security posture

---

## Security Control

**Definition:**  
A safeguard designed to reduce specific security risks.

Types:

### Physical Controls
Examples:
- Locks
- CCTV
- Security guards

### Technical Controls
Examples:
- Firewall
- MFA
- Antivirus

### Administrative Controls
Examples:
- Policies
- Authorization
- Separation of duties

---

## Security Framework

**Definition:**  
Guidelines used for building security plans to mitigate risks and threats.

Frameworks answer:

"What should an organization do?"

---

## Security Posture

**Definition:**  
An organization's ability to manage defense of critical assets and data and respond to change.

Improved by:

- Effective controls
- Security policies
- Risk management

---

## Risk

**Definition:**  
Anything that can impact the confidentiality, integrity, or availability of an asset.

Risk = Threat + Vulnerability + Impact

---

## Threat

**Definition:**  
Any circumstance or event that can negatively impact assets.

Examples:

- Malware
- Insider threats
- Phishing

---

## Vulnerability

**Definition:**  
A weakness that can be exploited by a threat actor.

Examples:

- Unpatched software
- Weak passwords
- Misconfiguration

---

## Separation of Duties

**Definition:**  
A security principle requiring critical actions to involve multiple people.

Purpose:

Prevent fraud and reduce misuse of privileges.

---

## Attack Surface Reduction

**Definition:**  
The practice of reducing possible entry points attackers can exploit.

Examples:

- Disable unnecessary services
- Remove unused software features

---

## Fail Securely

**Definition:**  
A principle where systems fail into a secure state when controls stop working.

Example:

Firewall failure → Block traffic

---

## Security by Obscurity

**Definition:**  
Relying on secrecy alone as a security mechanism.

OWASP recommendation:

Security should rely on strong controls, not hidden information.
